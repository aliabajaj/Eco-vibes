Anthropogenic factors are the leading cause of environmental damage

Climate Science
2026 Edition
8 Min Read

Greta Thunberg has often framed anthropogenic factors as a systemic crisis driven by unchecked economic growth and overconsumption. It can also be described as an environmental crisis driven by out-of-control economic growth and overindulgence of resources. The related concept of a human-dominated geological era, the "Anthropogene" (Anthropocene), was first introduced in 1922 by the Russian geologist and paleontologist Alexei Petrovich Pavlov. Anthropos, meaning human, and genic, coming from genesis, meaning origin or creation .

Nature is the epitome of beauty, calm and life, the mother to all. It can be described as the greatest source of wonderment, curiosity and joy and the only dominant source of things that make life worth living for and survival possible. As famously said by Mahatma Gandhi, an important figure in Indian history - “Earth (or nature) provides enough to satisfy every man's needs, but not every man's greed.”

As the economy grows, industries expand, humanity sees nothing but unwavering success whereas humanity is now bearing more and more responsibility for environmental damage and can also be known as the worst culprit of environmental degradation worldwide.

For ages man has been dazzled by material prosperity and has steadily improved technology, wealth and other such providers of physical and mental pleasures, but for a long time this exploitation was seemingly reasonable. A time when we used to hunt and gather, or a time when farming got popular and all those times in which material prosperity had not blinded the eyes of man with greed and the want for more. Greed is famously described as a strong, selfish desire to have more money, wealth, power, or possessions than you need.

It is now being slowly realized that man takes too much from nature and the exploitation is increasing day by day, and something must be done soon. In fact, "Humans are the only creatures on Earth that will cut down a tree, turn it into paper, then write 'save the trees' on it." Fyodor Dostoevsky once said that "Man, do not pride yourself on your superiority to the animals, for they are without sin, while you, with all your greatness, defile the earth wherever you appear..."

The industrial revolution

The industrial revolution is an example of exploitation and here a major change occurred. In the 18th century mostly everything owned by the people was handcrafted, knitted or was made by themselves. The industrial revolution took place in Great Britain where people discovered the various uses of coal. After this new discovery, The Industrial Revolution completely changed what human beings could make by introducing machine-powered factories. Instead of spinning yarn by hand, textile industries mass-produced cheap cotton and wool clothing for millions of people. Heavy industries used new methods to melt iron and steel, which were used to build massive steam engines, trains, and railway tracks.

In the 19th and early twentieth century many cities of Europe and the US were covered with shrouds of smoke. In some places a very inky atmosphere developed in which automobile drivers were forced to switch on their headlights at midday. In some parts of the west people started fighting and protesting against the industries, but the protesting and the fighting soon declined as many people had already joined the race of industrialism and blindly embraced its consequences.

Consequently, the many activities of today and the past done by man have made the world highly polluted and have degraded the pure quality of the environment, focusing on material possession and joy. At this point, Aparigriha, a Jain teaching, seems significant as it proposes non-possession and non-greed along with detachment from all material prosperity.

People who majorly contributed to the invention of industries:

Textile
  • James Hargreaves - invented the Spinning Jenny in 1764. It spun eight threads at once.
  • Richard Arkwright - invented the Water Frame in 1769. It made machines run on water power.
  • Edmund Cartwright - invented the Power Loom in 1785. It made weaving cloth much faster.
Energy and power
  • James Watt - improved the Steam Engine in 1776. His engine replaced muscle and wind power. It was used to run factory machines and trains.
Transportation
  • Richard Trevithick - built the first Steam Locomotive in 1804. It helped move goods across the country very fast.
Communication
  • Alexander Graham Bell - invented the Telephone in 1876.

The Bhopal Industrial disaster

Another suitable example is the Bhopal industrial disaster which took place in Bhopal, India. The Bhopal gas tragedy occurred on the night of December 2 - 3, 1984. In the tragedy at a pesticide plant owned by Union Carbide India Limited, water inadvertently entered a storage tank holding MIC (Methyl Isocyanate) during routine pipe maintenance. That caused the leakage of MIC, a very dangerous gas. The deadly gas silently stole through the air until the sleeping people woke up coughing with smoke in their eyes and throat or died. Now, that’s the human toll of the incident. Due to this tragedy, groundwater, soil and air were grossly contaminated for a long period of time. This leads to loss of biodiversity in that area and loss of plants. The catastrophe acted as a catalyst for environmental law in India. It prompted the passage of the Environment Protection Act, 1986, which established stricter regulatory frameworks for hazardous industries.

Today when people talk about this disaster they think of how many people died , what those people suffered and about the consequences they had to face but hardly anyone thinks about how much the environment was harmed and the consequences it had to face.

The impact of pesticides on the environment

A pesticide is any substance or mixture of substances used to prevent, destroy, repel, or control harmful pests. This is commonly used in farming to protect the crops, but have you ever wondered about its negative consequences? Pesticides used in agriculture can differ based on soil types, etc. It is composed of many ingredients which can be categorized into active ingredients and inert ingredients. The active ingredients basically just kill the pests whereas the inert ingredients are used to make the active ingredient help the product to work safely.

Overexploitation of natural resources

To begin we have to understand what natural resources are ? Natural resources are raw materials that are sources of energy for us and are obtained from the environment itself. Some examples can be petrol, rocks, sand, wind etc.. .These resources can be categorized into three sections -

01

Perpetually Renewable

Continuous natural energy sources that will last forever or are easily renewable. Examples include solar, wind, geothermal energy and tides/waves.

02

Intermediate Renewable

Temporary sources that remain renewable only if we do not overexploit and finish them quickly. Examples: freshwater, soil, crops.

03

Non-Renewable

Limited resources that cannot replenish in a suitable timeframe; mindful consumption is key. Examples: fossil fuels, metals, lithium.

It is being slowly realized that humans are exploiting these resources. The continuous over use of non renewable resources or intermediate renewable resources can strip us of these resources. Over use of these resources also harm the environment in several ways as well as strip other beings of access to these resources. Humans are deeply dependent on such resources but we must change that dependency to something more reliable and something which profits every being on this planet.

Mining for resources like coal and fossil fuels are harmful for the soil of the particular area and can cause loss of biodiversity as discussed before in this chapter.

The solution to this crisis is pretty simple. It is something we hear in our daily life but still refuse to adopt the method.

The first method is reduce,reuse and recycle. We can focus on reducing the usage of such resources by not wasting them and economizing them. Along with that we can reuse certain resources to diminish the demand of that particular resource in the society. Lastly, we can recycle these resources. This is the best option as this way we can use a certain amount of resources many times before finally discarding it. Agreed that recycling does have some bad effects like increasing microplastic but this is the least we can do to make our methods sustainable.

Cruelty to animals

Today when we mention cruelty to animals everybody relates it to poaching or hunting or animal abuse. They are not wrong but there is also another perspective to this term. Cruelty to animals is also mining, cutting down trees, over exploitation of resources and etc.. .When we do this, we just don’t harm the environment but we strip these animals of their homes and resources and that is real cruelty to animals. We are forgetting something we learn in kindergarten - to share and care. This world and environment is built to care for and to share not for personal gain. The environment is real - it feels everything. For many decades we have been forcing these animals to change their way of living.

A very suitable example is how we build train tracks in the middle of the forest. A railway line (India) split a forest in two parts due to which a species of apes called the hoolock gibbons which barely set foot on the ground were separated. They were separated from their family and other parts of the jungle until conservationists built an artificial canopy above the railway track. Now that is cruelty to animals. Separating them from their loved ones.

One measure taken to reduce animal cruelty is the prevention of animal cruelty act. The Prevention of Cruelty to Animals (PCA) Act, 1960 is India's foundational law protecting animals from unnecessary pain and suffering. It applies to all living creatures (other than humans) and establishes strict guidelines for animal ownership, labor, transportation, and scientific experiments. This law can only stop animal abuse whereas not other forms of cruelty to animals. Now that is up to us, not to the law or anyone but to us. From street animals to wild animals to pets and to factory farmed animals to insects. The protection of these animals is in our hands and not in any others. Our behaviour towards animals is currently justified as unprincipled and very ruthless and we must do something to change that.
